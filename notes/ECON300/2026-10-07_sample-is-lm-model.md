# IS-LM Model

## Key Terms
- **IS curve** — combinations of interest rate and output where the goods market is in equilibrium; slopes down because lower rates raise investment.
- **LM curve** — combinations where money supply equals money demand; slopes up because higher output raises money demand and so the rate.
- **Crowding out** — government borrowing raises the interest rate and reduces private investment.

## Notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Why does IS slope down? | A lower interest rate makes borrowing cheaper, so investment rises, and output rises through the multiplier. |
| What shifts IS? | Government spending, taxes, consumer confidence, and animal spirits (investment sentiment). |
| What shifts LM? | Changes in the money supply set by the central bank. An increase in the money supply shifts LM right and lowers the rate. |
| Liquidity trap | When the nominal rate is near zero, more money does not lower the rate further, so monetary policy loses power. |

## Summary
IS-LM finds the short-run rate and output where both goods and money markets clear. Fiscal policy shifts IS, monetary policy shifts LM. Professor flagged that the model holds prices fixed, which is why it fails to explain inflation episodes.
