# Fiscal Multipliers

## Key Terms
- **Spending multiplier** — change in output per dollar of initial spending; 1 / (1 − MPC) in the simple model with no taxes.
- **MPC** — marginal propensity to consume, the share of extra income that is spent.
- **Automatic stabilizers** — taxes and transfers that move with the cycle without new legislation.

## Notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Simple multiplier formula | With MPC = 0.8 the multiplier is 1 / (1 − 0.8) = 5. Each dollar of spending raises output by five dollars. |
| Add a proportional tax rate t | Multiplier becomes 1 / (1 − MPC(1 − t)). With MPC = 0.8 and t = 0.25 it falls to 2.5. |
| Tax-cut multiplier | Smaller in magnitude than the spending multiplier: MPC / (1 − MPC). Part of a tax cut is saved first. |
| Why real multipliers are smaller | Crowding out, imports leaking abroad, and forward-looking households who save a windfall. |
| Exam tip | Professor said expect a multiplier calculation on the midterm, with and without a tax rate. |

## Summary
The multiplier amplifies spending because one person's spending is another person's income. Taxes, saving, and imports each reduce it. Know both formulas and be able to explain why the tax-cut multiplier is smaller.
