# Elasticity and Consumer Surplus

## Key Terms
- **Price elasticity of demand** — percent change in quantity demanded divided by percent change in price.
- **Consumer surplus** — the area between the demand curve and the price, up to the quantity bought.
- **Cross-price elasticity** — responsiveness of demand for one good to the price of another; positive for substitutes.

## Notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| Elastic vs inelastic | Elastic if |E| > 1: raising price lowers total revenue. Inelastic if |E| < 1: raising price raises revenue. |
| What makes demand more elastic? | Many close substitutes, a large share of budget, and a longer time horizon. |
| Midpoint formula | Use average price and quantity as the base so the elasticity is the same in either direction. |
| Consumer surplus calc | For linear demand it is a triangle: 1/2 × base × height. Example: max willingness to pay $10, price $4, quantity 6 gives $18. |

## Summary
Elasticity tells a firm whether a price increase helps revenue. Consumer surplus measures buyer gains from trade and shrinks when price rises. Problem set 3 uses the midpoint formula.
