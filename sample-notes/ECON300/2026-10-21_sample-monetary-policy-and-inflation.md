# Monetary Policy and Inflation

## Key Terms
- **Taylor rule** — a guide for the policy rate: i = π + r* + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5(output gap).
- **Phillips curve** — the relationship between inflation and unemployment; shifts with expectations.
- **Anchored expectations** — when the public expects inflation to return to target, which makes disinflation cheaper.

## Notes
| Cue | Notes |
|---|---|
| How does the Fed tighten? | Raises the target for the federal funds rate; reduces investment and consumption, cooling demand. |
| Taylor rule intuition | Raise the rate more than one-for-one when inflation rises (the Taylor principle), otherwise real rates fall and inflation accelerates. |
| Short-run vs long-run Phillips curve | Short run: a tradeoff. Long run: vertical at the natural rate of unemployment. |
| 1970s lesson | Expectations became unanchored, so the Phillips curve shifted up and disinflation required a deep recession. |

## Summary
Monetary policy works with lags of 12 to 18 months. Credibility matters: if expectations are anchored, the central bank can lower inflation with less unemployment. Reading for next class: the Volcker disinflation case.
